About Us

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), safe blood transfusion is a universal human right. Safe blood is fully screened, free from infectious diseases, properly stored, correctly labeled, and used within a specified time period.

Blood transfusion services are an integral part of the healthcare system. Blood transfusion is an indispensable, potentially life-saving medical intervention. Millions of lives are saved each year through blood transfusions.

The provision of safe blood and blood components is the responsibility of the national healthcare system. Safety of blood has assumed greater importance in developing countries where HIV, hepatitis B, and hepatitis C are major public health concerns.

The National Blood Transfusion Program was started in 1942 at the Provincial Headquarter Lahore. At the time of independence in 1947, the system was inherited and continued under the same structure.

Pakistan collects approximately 2.7 million blood donations annually from nearly 650 blood centers.

The Institute has shifted blood screening from ICT to CLIA technology to achieve higher
accuracy. After technical consultations and stakeholder meetings, CLIA-based screening has
been introduced across Punjab.
